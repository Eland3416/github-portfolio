A LITTLE ABOUT ME

Curious by nature.
Developer by design.

I'm working toward becoming a software developer who can contribute to thoughtful, reliable, and useful technology.

✳Always learning. Always building.

I am a Computer Science student at Lamar University, preparing to graduate on December 11, 2026. My goal is to begin a career in software development where I can continue learning, solve interesting problems, and build applications that people can actually use. Throughout college, I have developed an interest in programming, networking, operating systems, and artificial intelligence.

My coursework has given me hands-on experience with Java, Python, C++, SQL, and several development tools. I have worked with Linux, Docker, Git, and networking simulation software such as ns-3. One project I particularly enjoyed involved comparing TCP congestion control algorithms and examining how network conditions affect performance. I have also worked on sorting algorithms, machine learning exercises, and an AI Study Assistant designed to help students review computer science concepts.

Outside of school, I currently work as a Physical Therapy Technician at Moreau Physical Therapy and as a Front Desk Attendant at CHRISTUS Health and Wellness Center. At Moreau, I help guide patients through exercises and maintain a clean clinical environment. At CHRISTUS, I greet members and help keep the facility organized and welcoming. Both positions have strengthened my communication skills, attention to detail, and ability to work with different people.

I also have several years of experience umpiring youth baseball games for local leagues and travel tournaments. Umpiring taught me how to make decisions under pressure, stay focused, and communicate clearly when situations become challenging. Those skills are useful in programming too, especially when troubleshooting problems or working with a team.

What I enjoy most about computer science is the process of figuring things out. I like taking a problem that seems complicated, breaking it into smaller pieces, and gradually building a solution. I know there is still plenty for me to learn, particularly in full-stack development, APIs, and professional software engineering practices. This portfolio is a way to showcase the progress I have made while continuing to improve my skills and prepare for my first software development role.
